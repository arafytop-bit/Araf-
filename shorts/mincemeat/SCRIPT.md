## Facts (and sources)
- Operation Mincemeat, 1943: British deception to make the Axis expect landings in Greece and Sardinia instead of
  Sicily (Operation Husky, July 1943). Planned by Lt Cdr Ewen Montagu (Naval Intelligence) and Flt Lt Charles
  Cholmondeley (RAF, MI5). Source: Ben Macintyre, *Operation Mincemeat* (2010); UK National Archives file
  "Operation Mincemeat" (ADM 223/794, CAB 154/67); Montagu, *The Man Who Never Was* (1953).
- The body was Glyndwr Michael, a homeless Welshman who died after eating rat poison containing phosphorus
  (Macintyre 2010; Commonwealth War Graves Commission record).
- Disguised as "Major William Martin, Royal Marines", with a briefcase chained to him holding a letter from Gen. Nye
  to Gen. Alexander (CWGC; National Archives).
- "Pocket litter": photo of fiancée "Pam" (actually MI5 clerk Jean Leslie), two love letters, a jeweller's receipt
  for an engagement ring, theatre ticket stubs, a letter from Lloyds Bank about an overdraft (Macintyre 2010;
  Imperial War Museum).
- 30 April 1943: released from submarine HMS Seraph off Huelva, Spain; found by a local sardine fisherman, José
  Antonio Rey María. Spanish officials let German agents copy the documents (Macintyre 2010).
- Hitler believed it; German forces were reinforced in Greece/Sardinia (e.g. 1st Panzer Division moved to Greece).
  Allies invaded Sicily on 10 July 1943 (Macintyre 2010; IWM).
- Grave in Huelva (Nuestra Señora cemetery) read "William Martin"; in 1998 the CWGC added "Glyndwr Michael served
  as Major William Martin, RM" (CWGC).
